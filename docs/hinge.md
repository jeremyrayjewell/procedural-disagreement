# The hinge, 1:52.5–2:20.0

The passage between the individual-case half of the film and the systemic half. Its job is to
leave the individual case: to turn a waste landscape into the system that produces and routes
it, and then to stop.

Nothing after 2:20.0 is authored. This document states what must be true at 2:20.0.

The code is in `procedural_disagreement/core/hinge.py` (what is on the station level and what
of it can be seen when) and `core/choreography.py` (`hinge_rise`, `hinge_lag`, `hinge_pitch`:
the camera).

## What the film had not shown

The chutes hang from a ceiling that has only ever been darkness overhead. Above it is the level
they hang from. On that level:

- **Trunks.** Seventeen straight parallel lines, 24 m apart, each the length of the field.
- **Mains.** Four heavier lines across them, 96 m apart.
- **Stations.** Over every chute, a square unit, set square to the trunks whatever way its
  chute is turned, joined to its own trunk by a short spur.

Seen from high enough, the level also has a hierarchy and regions, the way the routing of an
integrated-circuit die does. All of it is disposal plant; it is only very regular.

- **Heavy buses.** One trunk is 4 m wide and one main is 5 m wide: a very small number of
  heavy lines over the ordinary trunks (1.6 m) and mains (2.6 m).
- **A fine local layer.** In three rectangular regions, runs 0.6 m wide at a 4 m pitch, along
  the trunks in one region and across them in two. They are lower than everything they run
  between.
- **Cell arrays.** In two regions, regular arrays of small low holding cells (3.2 × 5.2 m at
  a 6 × 8 m pitch in one, 5 × 5 m at 8 × 8 m in the other).
- **Bays.** About two stations in five have two bays either side, in line along the trunk, so
  from a distance a station is a short row of cells and not one square.
- **Junctions.** Where each spur meets its trunk a block stands a little proud of both.
- **A field.** The level keeps its tone inside a broad rectangle square to the trunks and goes
  dark over 50 m outside it. No edge is drawn.

The regions lie ahead of where the camera comes up and are not symmetric. Nothing is added to
the one trunk the film is left with after the cut, so the 2:15–2:20 image is unchanged.

The resemblance is meant to be noticed, not shown: still plainly plant at 2:00–2:05, routing
conspicuous by 2:05–2:09, most die-like at about 2:11–2:13 (camera 100–140 m up, 70–72° below
the horizon, still moving, refuse visible through the structure), then pattern, then the cut.

From below, the chutes look scattered: each stands up to 8.5 m off the grid in each direction.
From above, every one of them hangs off a perfectly regular system. The disorder was in where
things were dropped, not in the thing that dropped them.

Nothing on the level can be seen from below or from inside the chamber, and none of it is lit
before 1:57.5, so no frame before 1:52.5 changes.

## The passage

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1:52.5 | The locked flyover continues without a seam: same line, same speed (6 m/s), same pitch. The camera begins to rise, from no rate at all. |
| 1:52.5–1:57.5 | It climbs 3.8 m, still under the ceiling, still over refuse. |
| 1:57.5 | The ceiling stops being a ceiling. The level above begins to come out of the dark, seen first from underneath. |
| 1:58.6 | The camera passes up through the level, between two trunks. |
| 2:00.0 | It is 4 m above the level, looking along it: trunks, a main, units, and the waste underneath, through the gaps. The level is at full tone. |
| 2:00–2:15 | Forward speed eases from 6 m/s to 1.5 m/s. The climb accelerates: 33 m up at 2:05, 87 m at 2:10, 170 m at 2:15. The camera tips from 14° below the horizon to 72°, which it reaches at 2:13 and holds. |
| 2:15.0 | **Hard cut**, the second in the film. |
| 2:15–2:20 | One trunk and one station in darkness, from a camera that does not move. Nothing moves. |

**How the landfill becomes organisation and not just more landfill.** The camera does not climb
over a wider field of refuse: it goes through the surface the chutes hang from, and what it
finds above is a different kind of thing. Refuse is then only seen through the gaps in a
regular structure, and from further and further away.

**What disappears, in order.** Small debris and single limbs first (unreadable by about 2:02,
40 m up). Then the larger pieces and the strata in the bench faces (by about 2:06). Then the
benches and ridges themselves: by 2:10 the terrain is an irregular dark texture under the grid.
The chutes are hidden under their units from the moment the camera is above them.

**What becomes visible.** The trunks' spacing and straightness; the mains; that every station
is square to the grid; that the stations, which looked scattered, are each a short spur from a
line. By 2:15 the frame is a field of parallel bands and repeated blocks over an irregular
ground: order above disorder, in geometry only.

**The cut.** From a camera 170 m up, climbing at 20 m/s, to one that is standing still 2.6 m
above the level. The ceiling is back, so there is no waste. Everything on the level is unlit
except one trunk, fading along its length to nothing by about 190 m, and one station unit on
it 46 m ahead with its spur. The rest of the frame is black.

**Density, 2:15–2:20.** Three lit objects' worth of structure: one line, one block, one short
connection. No motion of any kind.

## Final-render annotations (not implemented)

1:52.5–2:15 is where a lit engine can do most for the scale change:

- atmospheric depth and volumetric extinction, increasing with altitude;
- aerial haze thickening between the camera and the ground;
- distant station lighting, so the far field reads as points and lines and not as fall-off;
- material detail disappearing naturally with distance;
- selective emissive networks along trunks and mains;
- audio-responsive visibility of the infrastructure.

The semiconductor association can be strengthened in the final render through material and
lighting, not more geometry:

- dark silicon-like structural surfaces;
- restrained metallic interconnect materials, with hierarchical roughness and specular response;
- subtle copper- and aluminium-like variation between routing levels;
- small highlights at the junction blocks;
- sparse emissive, data-like activity along selected routes;
- indirect light revealing the layering of the routing;
- atmospheric separation between the ordered level and the refuse below.

The association wanted is advanced manufactured infrastructure. Not neon, not a glowing
circuit board, not a science-fiction computer. No logo, text, wafer, package or any real die's
layout is reproduced, now or later.

In the final profile the system's organisation may become legible partly through lighting and
atmosphere. The preview communicates it through geometry and distance alone, and does.

The preview stands in for distance with a tone that falls to nothing 260 m from the middle of
the view and a black shell beyond; a lit engine should replace both with real extinction.
