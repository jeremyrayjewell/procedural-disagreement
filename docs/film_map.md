# Film map: the whole track, and what exists so far

A planning document. Nothing here is implemented beyond what the cue file already contains.

Sources: `analysis/audio_features.json` (the master), the isolated vocal `onlyvox1.wav`, which is
sample-aligned with it, and the raw vocal takes in the song's folder. Times are track time. The
lyric layer below is a summary of [lyric_alignment.md](lyric_alignment.md), which has the word
timings, the evidence and the uncertainties.

## The track at a glance

- **Duration:** 4:40.00 (280.000 s). Audible material ends at about 4:37; the last three seconds are silence.
- **Tempo:** 96 BPM throughout, on a fixed grid (beat 0.625 s, bar 2.5 s). Phrases are four bars: every 10 s is a phrase boundary, from 0:00 to the end. The grid is loose only in the first 40 s and the last 25 s, where there is no steady pulse to lock to.
- **Harmony:** centred on F / C for the whole song until 4:15, where it moves to a new area (D# / A#) for the coda. No harmonic change marks any earlier boundary; structure is carried by texture, bass and voice.
- **Shape:** two large halves and a coda. The second half (2:15–4:15) reprises the first half's music (0:15–2:15) almost bar for bar, with the voice distributed differently.

What the lyric layer adds to the musical structure:

- **The refrain is the song's background condition.** It is said eleven times. Nine of them are filtered, and the songwriter means those as background noise, as conditions, not as someone speaking. It is only heard plainly at 4:15, as the last words of the song.
- **The two halves do not carry the same words.** Verse 1 (A) is never sung again. Over the reprise of its music, the second half has only the refrain, looped. The diptych is musical, not lyrical: in the second half the statement is gone and only the conditions remain.
- **The song names the film's own objects.** "Trunk" (0:43.0, 0:53.0) and "stump" (0:48.6, 0:58.6) are in verse 1; "landfill" (1:36.15, 1:46.15) in verse 2. The stump in particular is sung before it exists on screen, again as it is made, and is then the thing the phantom-load and recruitment passages turn on.
- **Each verse is one couplet said twice.** So every image has a second chance ten seconds later.
- **There is no voice at all in the film's longest and loudest stretch** (3:00–4:15, 75 s), and the refrain in full voice is what follows it.
- **Stressed syllables worth knowing:** "–sive" (0:42.5, 0:52.5) and "trunk" (0:43.0, 0:53.0); the swell on "the" into "stump" (0:47.7 into 0:48.6; 0:57.7 into 0:58.6); "land–" (1:36.15, 1:46.15) and "–fill" (1:36.9, 1:46.9); "scrawls" and "bars" in every refrain (first at 1:21.65 and 1:24.1).
- **The only real pause in the lyric** is 0.4 s inside the refrain, before its second line (first at 1:22.1–1:22.5).

Recorded but deliberately left out of the song (wording from speech recognition on a raw take, unchecked; context only): "There never was a presence / quite
like this absence", "There never was compulsion / quite like this prohibition", and "But for now it
seems worthy / of minor controversy / [and / in] amputation". The first of these describes the phantom
and absent-attractor passages closely, and the last is the title; neither is sung in the song.

No voice: 0:00–0:40 (40 s), 1:00–1:20, 1:24.6–1:30, 1:49.4–2:20 (30.6 s), and **3:00–4:15 (75 s)**.

## Undeveloped regions and what each is for

Broad dramatic function only.

| Region | Length | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:35 | 35 s | **Before.** The system whole and unremarked. The sparse hard transients of the first ten seconds are the only events: instruments being readied, or the room coming on. It must hand over to the approach that is already under way at 0:35, so it ends on the same camera, already moving. |
| 1:52.5–2:15 | 22.5 s | **Leaving.** The flyover has made its point by 1:50. The music carries on full for one phrase and then drains. This is the way out of the landfill: either the field simply continues until it dissolves, or the film climbs back toward the chamber. The energy should fall with the bass from 2:00. |
| 2:15–2:20 | 5 s | **Break.** A hard reset. The only place before 4:15 where the track itself stops. A cut, a blackout, or a return. |
| 2:20–3:00 | 40 s | **Conditions.** The opening music again, but verse 1 is gone: nobody is giving an account. What is left is the filtered refrain, eight times, one statement every two bars, as background: a scrawled acknowledgement and a row of medals as the standing state of the place. Quiet, administrative, repetitive. This suits picture that is environment and not event; the five-second loop with its one pause is available as a metre for it. |
| 3:00–4:15 | 75 s | **The system without a subject.** The loud music of the amputation and the landfill returns with no voice at all, for longer than anything else in the song. This is where scale and repetition belong: many stations, many procedures, disagreement as a normal operating condition. It has its own internal landmarks: the entrance at 3:00, the bass-out phrase at 3:20–3:30 (the twin of the absent attractor), and a sustained peak at 4:00–4:15 that has no counterpart in the first half. That peak is the film's climax. |
| 4:15–4:25 | 10 s | **Withdrawal, and the refrain heard plainly.** The largest drop in the track, a new harmony, and the two lines that have been background since 1:20, now in the foreground and in full voice. Whatever the 75 s built is switched off or left. What remains is the scrawl and the medals. |
| 4:25–4:40 | 15 s | **Residue.** Isolated hard transients over almost nothing, echoing the first ten seconds. A closing image that answers the opening; then three seconds of silence to end on. |

Two structural facts worth designing around:

- **The song is a diptych.** Because 2:15–4:15 repeats 0:15–2:15, the second half can rhyme with the first shot for shot, or deliberately refuse to. Either is a decision about the whole film, and it should be made before any single scene in the second half.
- **The landfill is at the centre, not the end.** Its flyover finishes at 1:50, 39% of the way through. Everything after it has to be something other than a bigger landfill.

## Where the final-render layer will matter most

The planned audio-responsive layer (screens, lighting, material and atmospheric response) is not implemented. These are the places it would carry the most weight.

| Region | Layer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | Lighting | Isolated transients over near-silence: single lights or panels coming on one at a time would be legible and would establish the layer's grammar before anything happens. |
| 0:58.6 | Lighting, material or screen accent | The second sung "stump", with the stump already on screen. No structural choreography is to be added; a subtle accent is possible. |
| 1:00.0 | Screens, lighting | The highs open by 8 dB. The natural moment for the back-wall surfaces to wake for the first time, as the system branches. |
| 1:20–1:30 | Material, texture | The filtered refrain (1:20.0–1:24.6) is an environmental condition, not an announcement: whatever answers it behaves like the room. Bass out, brightness doubled. The absent attractor is subtle in geometry; fine surface activity with no low-frequency light would support it without pointing at anything. |
| 1:36.9–1:52.5 | Atmosphere, lighting, environmental detail | The landfill needs depth that flat shading cannot give: haze, pools of light under chutes, distant chutes lit selectively. Its bureaucratic variant (panels activating along the disposal route) belongs to 1:32–1:37 above. |
| 2:15–2:20 | Lighting | The breakdown: the layer going dark is as strong as anything it can do lit. |
| 2:20–3:00 | Screens, texture | The filtered refrain, eight times, as background conditions: a natural match for ambient surface behaviour and a poor one for events. The lyric licenses two kinds of mark. *Scrawls*: non-linguistic irregular marks, scratches, traces, scribble-like surface patterns. *Medal bars*: ordered rectilinear bands, serial blocks, ranked repeated modules. No readable writing. Here they should appear mainly as environmental, material and screen behaviour, not as eight new narrative events. High-frequency surface detail suits the voice's thin, bright sound. |
| 3:00–4:15 | Everything | 75 s of loud, voiceless music. This is where the screens, secondary lighting and environmental expansion should be at full strength, and where the high-power render earns its cost. The 3:20–3:30 bass-out and the 4:00–4:15 peak give it shape. |
| 4:15.0 | Lighting, atmosphere | The drop: the whole layer withdrawing at once. |
| 4:15–4:25 | Screens, material | The refrain in full voice. Reserved: the scrawl and bar vocabulary may be made much more explicit here. |
| 4:25–4:37 | Lighting | The coda's transients, one light each, mirroring the opening. |

## Caveats

- The words are the songwriter's. Word timings are measured and carry the uncertainties given in the alignment document (typically ±0.05 s on stressed syllables, ±0.15 s on short unstressed words).
- The songwriter has said the filtered refrain is background noise, conditions; that the unused lines of the longer take are deliberately absent; and that "the offensive trunk" is the trunk that offends one's sensibilities. Readings beyond those three statements are mine.
- The cues at 0:40–1:00 were chosen from the instrumental features before any vocal file was found. Several of them turn out to sit on meaningful words by accident; the alignment document lists each one.
